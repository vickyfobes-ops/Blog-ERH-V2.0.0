# ERH editorial visual direction

## Default image system

Use visually rich residential kitchen scenes, not isolated object studies. Show enough of the room to judge cabinetry, ceiling, cooking appliance, counters, lighting, traffic space, and the hood's architectural scale.

Prioritize:

- exterior silhouette and overall proportion;
- copper, brass, stainless steel, or zinc appearance;
- aged, brushed, hammered, smooth, darkened, or other currently approved finish behavior;
- crowns, aprons, trim joints, straps, rivets, pot rails, and restrained edge details;
- the relationship between the hood and cabinetry, stone, tile, wood, lighting, and hardware;
- believable U.S. residential design and construction context.

## Prohibited defaults

Unless the user explicitly requests a technical illustration, do not generate:

- cutaways or cross-sections;
- transparent or missing shells;
- exploded assemblies;
- exposed ducts, blowers, motors, filters, wires, or fabricated inner chambers;
- anatomy labels or engineering details that are not documented by the exact ERH product;
- a competitor logo, watermark, product photo, or distinctive composition.

## Product references

Inspect current ERH product pages or approved ERH images before describing or visualizing a named model. Use competitor sites only to understand category presentation and market conventions, never as a source of ERH facts or image assets.

If a generated hood only references an exterior profile, say so. Do not call it the exact model or customer project. Do not imply that a generated kitchen is an installed ERH project.

## Standard six-image package

For a full buying or design guide, prefer:

1. Featured image: complete aspirational kitchen with the article's primary hood concept.
2. Proportion scene: another kitchen illustrating visual scale or mounting context.
3. Material scene: a full kitchen emphasizing one metal and finish.
4. Selection scene: finish samples or design materials photographed in a residential setting.
5. Silhouette scene: a contrasting architectural form in a different kitchen style.
6. Craft scene: a full kitchen where straps, rivets, apron, crown, hammering, or trim can be inspected.

Vary kitchen style, cabinetry color, materials, and lighting while maintaining a coherent premium residential aesthetic. Avoid six near-duplicate rooms.

## Disclosure and captions

Include one clear early disclosure when generated visuals are used, for example:

`The kitchen interiors in this guide are AI-generated design visualizations, not customer-project photography.`

Use captions to explain the decision the image supports. Avoid promotional filler. Example:

`Copper finish visualization in a warm natural-material kitchen; use physical ERH samples to approve actual color, texture, and finish behavior.`

## Alt text

Describe visible facts and context in one readable sentence. Include a model name only when the relationship is accurate and useful. Do not repeat the caption or stuff search terms.
